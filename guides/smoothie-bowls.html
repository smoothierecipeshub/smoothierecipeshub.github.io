Smoothie Bowls

A smoothie bowl is a smoothie thick enough to eat with a spoon, served in a bowl and topped with fruit, granola, nuts, and seeds. It is more filling than a drinkable smoothie, because the thickness and the toppings slow you down and make you chew. Smoothie bowls look impressive, but they are simple to make: the key is using less liquid and more frozen fruit than a normal smoothie. This guide explains how to get the thickness right, which toppings work best, and how to build a bowl that is a proper meal.

Getting the thickness right

A smoothie bowl needs to be much thicker than a drinkable smoothie. Use frozen fruit as the base, about two cups, and add only a quarter to half a cup of liquid. Start with a small amount of liquid and add more only if the blender struggles. A high-powered blender works best, but you can manage with a normal blender by stopping to stir and pushing the fruit down. The finished bowl should hold its shape when you scoop it, like soft-serve ice cream. If it is too thin, add more frozen fruit or a few ice cubes and blend again.

The best bases

Frozen banana is the best base for most bowls: it is sweet, creamy, and thick. Frozen mango and frozen berries also work well. A mix of frozen banana and frozen berries gives a good balance of sweetness and colour. Add a spoonful of Greek yoghurt or a quarter of an avocado for extra creaminess. Avoid using too much fresh fruit, because it adds liquid and makes the bowl thin. Keep bags of frozen fruit in the freezer so you can make a bowl any time without planning ahead.

Toppings that work

Toppings add texture, flavour, and nutrition. Good choices are granola, sliced banana, berries, chopped nuts, seeds, coconut flakes, and a drizzle of nut butter. Keep portions in check: a tablespoon of nuts and two tablespoons of granola are enough, because toppings add calories quickly. Arrange toppings in neat rows for a good look, or scatter them for a casual bowl. Avoid adding too much honey or chocolate, which turns a healthy bowl into a dessert. The best bowls balance sweet fruit with crunchy, savoury toppings.

Three smoothie bowl recipes

A berry bowl blends two frozen bananas, a cup of frozen mixed berries, and a quarter cup of milk, topped with granola, sliced strawberries, and chia seeds. A tropical bowl blends one frozen banana, a cup of frozen mango, half a cup of frozen pineapple, and a quarter cup of coconut milk, topped with coconut flakes, kiwi, and chopped macadamia nuts. A green bowl blends one frozen banana, a cup of frozen mango, a handful of spinach, and a quarter cup of milk, topped with sliced banana, blueberries, and pumpkin seeds.

Bowls for the weekend

Smoothie bowls are best saved for the weekend, when you have time to enjoy them slowly. Make the base thick, pour it into a bowl, and arrange the toppings in neat rows, which is half the pleasure of a smoothie bowl. Sit down at the table and eat it with a spoon, rather than rushing it at the counter. Try a new combination each weekend, such as a tropical bowl one week and a chocolate bowl the next, and take a photo of your favourite. A smoothie bowl is a small weekend ritual that makes healthy eating feel like a treat rather than a chore.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my smoothie bowl too runny?

You probably used too much liquid. Use frozen fruit and only a quarter cup of liquid, adding more a spoonful at a time.

Are smoothie bowls healthy?

They can be, if the toppings are kept in check. Granola, nuts, and honey add calories quickly, so measure them.

Can I make a smoothie bowl without a high-powered blender?

Yes. Use a normal blender, stop often to stir, and add liquid slowly. It takes longer, but it works.
