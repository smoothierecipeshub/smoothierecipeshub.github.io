Low-Sugar Smoothies

Smoothies can be surprisingly high in sugar, because fruit is sweet and it is easy to blend several servings at once. A smoothie with two bananas, a cup of mango, and a cup of juice can contain more sugar than a can of fizzy drink. But low-sugar smoothies are easy to make: use berries and vegetables, choose water or unsweetened milk, and skip juice and honey. This guide explains how to cut sugar without losing flavour, and gives recipes that are light, fresh, and satisfying.

Where smoothie sugar comes from

Most smoothie sugar comes from fruit, and from juice and sweeteners. A banana has about twelve grams of sugar, a cup of mango about twenty-three grams, and a cup of orange juice about twenty-one grams. These add up fast. Honey adds about six grams per teaspoon, and sweetened yoghurt can add ten grams or more. None of these is a problem in small amounts, but a smoothie that combines several of them can easily contain fifty grams of sugar or more. The fix is to use fewer high-sugar fruits, more vegetables and berries, and unsweetened liquids.

Low-sugar fruits and vegetables

The lowest-sugar fruits for smoothies are berries: raspberries, strawberries, and blackberries all have five to seven grams of sugar per cup. Green apple is lower in sugar than a banana or mango. Avocado has almost no sugar and adds creaminess. Vegetables are the best low-sugar base: spinach, cucumber, celery, and courgette add bulk and nutrients with almost no sugar. Lemon and lime juice add flavour without sugar, and herbs like mint and basil add freshness for free.

Three low-sugar recipes

A berry green smoothie blends a cup of mixed berries, a large handful of spinach, 150 grams of Greek yoghurt, and 200 millilitres of water. It has about 150 calories and 10 grams of sugar. A cucumber and apple smoothie blends half a cucumber, one green apple, a handful of spinach, the juice of half a lemon, and a cup of water. A chocolate avocado smoothie blends half an avocado, one tablespoon of cocoa powder, 200 millilitres of unsweetened almond milk, and a handful of ice, with a few drops of stevia if you want it sweeter.

Sweetening without sugar

If a low-sugar smoothie tastes too tart, there are ways to sweeten it without sugar. A few drops of stevia or a small amount of monk fruit sweetener add sweetness with no calories. Vanilla extract adds a sweet flavour without sugar. Cinnamon tricks the taste buds into sensing sweetness. A small amount of ripe banana, half rather than a whole one, adds sweetness for fewer grams of sugar. Over time, your taste buds adjust: after a few weeks of low-sugar smoothies, a drink that once tasted tart will taste just right.

Retraining your taste buds

If you are used to sweet smoothies, low-sugar versions can taste tart at first, but your taste buds adjust within a few weeks. Start by cutting the sweet ingredients in half and adding more berries or vegetables, and use a squeeze of lemon or a little vanilla to add flavour without sugar. After a week or two, the lower-sugar smoothie will taste normal, and a very sweet smoothie will taste too sweet. This change is worth making, because it helps you enjoy fruit for its flavour rather than its sugar, and it keeps your energy steadier through the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much sugar is in a typical smoothie?

A fruit smoothie can have 30 to 50 grams of sugar. A low-sugar smoothie made with berries and vegetables has about 10 to 15 grams.

Are smoothies okay for people with diabetes?

They can be, if they are low in sugar and high in fibre and protein. Use berries, vegetables, and unsweetened milk, and check with your doctor.

Is honey a healthy sweetener for smoothies?

Honey is natural, but it is still sugar. A teaspoon adds about six grams of sugar, so use it sparingly or skip it.
