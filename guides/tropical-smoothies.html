Tropical Smoothies

Tropical smoothies taste like sunshine: sweet mango, sharp pineapple, creamy coconut, and a squeeze of lime. They are popular for a reason, because tropical fruit is naturally sweet and blends into a thick, smooth drink with very little effort. The challenge is keeping them from becoming too sweet or too high in calories. This guide gives recipes that balance tropical sweetness with lighter ingredients, and explains how to use fresh, frozen, and tinned fruit well.

The best tropical fruits

Mango is the star of tropical smoothies: it is sweet, creamy, and blends completely smooth. Pineapple adds sharpness and freshness, and a little goes a long way. Papaya is mild and creamy, and banana adds thickness. Coconut milk adds richness, but it is high in fat, so use a small amount or choose light coconut milk. Lime juice is the secret ingredient in most tropical smoothies: a squeeze brightens the sweetness and stops the drink tasting flat. Passion fruit adds a sharp, fragrant note, but use it sparingly because it is very tart.

Fresh, frozen, or tinned

Frozen mango and pineapple are the most practical choice for smoothies: they are cheap, available all year, and make the drink thick and cold. Fresh mango is best when it is ripe and soft; an unripe mango is sour and fibrous. Tinned pineapple in juice is acceptable, but avoid tinned fruit in syrup, which adds a lot of sugar. Drain tinned fruit well before blending. If you use fresh fruit, add a handful of ice to chill and thicken the smoothie. Keep bags of frozen mango and pineapple in the freezer so a tropical smoothie is always possible.

Three tropical recipes

A mango and pineapple smoothie blends a cup of frozen mango, half a cup of frozen pineapple, one banana, and 200 millilitres of coconut water, with a squeeze of lime. A coconut mango smoothie blends a cup of frozen mango, 100 millilitres of light coconut milk, 100 millilitres of milk, and half a banana. A pineapple and lime smoothie blends a cup of frozen pineapple, one banana, a handful of spinach, the juice of half a lime, and 200 millilitres of water. Each makes one large serving of about 200 to 300 calories.

Keeping tropical smoothies balanced

Tropical fruit is high in natural sugar, so balance it with lighter ingredients. Use water or coconut water instead of fruit juice, add a handful of spinach or cucumber for bulk, and keep the portion to one or two servings of fruit. A squeeze of lime or lemon cuts the sweetness and adds freshness. If you want a meal rather than a snack, add Greek yoghurt or protein powder for staying power. Avoid adding honey to a tropical smoothie: mango and pineapple are already sweet enough.

A taste of summer

Tropical smoothies are at their best in summer, when the weather is hot and a cold, sweet drink is most welcome. Keep bags of frozen mango and pineapple in the freezer, and a bottle of coconut water in the fridge, so a tropical smoothie is always quick to make. Serve it in a tall glass with ice and a wedge of lime, and drink it outside if you can. For a party, make a large batch in a jug and let guests help themselves. A tropical smoothie is a simple pleasure, and it tastes better when you slow down and enjoy it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is coconut milk healthy in a smoothie?

Full-fat coconut milk is high in saturated fat, so use a small amount or choose light coconut milk. Coconut water is a lighter alternative.

Can I use tinned pineapple in a smoothie?

Yes, if it is tinned in juice rather than syrup. Drain it well and use it in place of fresh or frozen pineapple.

Why does my tropical smoothie taste too sweet?

Tropical fruit is naturally sweet. Add lime juice, a handful of spinach, or some cucumber to balance it, and skip any added honey.
